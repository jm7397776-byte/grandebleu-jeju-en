But there's a quieter side to Jeju Island most first-time visitors miss — one that has less to do with tour buses and more to do with basalt sea cliffs, hexagonal columns, and a glass of chilled white wine while the sun drops behind Hallasan.

This is a straight review of the Grande Bleu Jeju yacht, written for travellers who value the details.

Why a Catamaran, and Why Now

Most Jeju boat tours run on single-hull vessels — the kind that pitch and roll enough to test even seasoned travellers. The Grande Bleu is different. Its 17.1-metre twin-hull design sits flat on the water, which is why it's the only yacht in Jeju cleared to sail without life jackets on deck. For a couple in their sixties, that stability isn't a luxury detail — it's the difference between a memorable afternoon and a queasy one.

Spring (March to May) is arguably the finest window. The winter winds ease off, the sea calms, and the light along Seogwipo's southern coast turns soft and slightly golden by late afternoon. It's the slow side of K-culture — no crowds, no queues, no soundtrack.

The Coastline You Came For

The 60-minute route hugs Seogwipo's UNESCO-recognised volcanic coast. It's the sort of geology that photographs well without needing a filter.

  • Elephant Rock (Kkokkiribawi) — a cliff formation that genuinely does look like an elephant drinking
  • Wolpyeong Basalt Columns — hexagonal sea cliffs from the same volcanic family as Giant's Causeway
  • Wolpyeong Sea Cave — waves fold into a hollow in the cliff
  • Jingotnae Sea Canyon — vertical walls dropping straight into deep water
  • Pangpaengideok — a flat reef plateau just above sea level
  • Large Sea Cave (Keunbada Donggul) — the biggest on this stretch of coast

The captain slows near each landmark. There's no rushed narration — just enough context to point out what you're looking at, then quiet.

What's Actually On Board (And Free)

This is where the value quietly stacks up. Everything below is included, unlimited, with no extra charge:

  • Draft beer (Korean fresh brew)
  • Red and white wine
  • Freshly squeezed Jeju tangerine juice
  • Bottled water
  • Jeju local snacks and traditional cookies
  • Korean ramyeon, cooked onboard

For a couple, that's roughly two glasses of wine each, a bowl of noodles at sea, and a view of basalt columns catching the last light — for the price of a mid-range London lunch.

Fishing, If You Fancy It

Every tour includes a rod, bait, and a short window to fish off the deck. Local catch runs to rockfish, scorpionfish, filefish, and puffer. Beginners manage fine — the crew rigs everything — and catch-and-release is standard. It's an oddly companionable activity for couples, and the sort of small detail that lifts this above a standard sightseeing sail.

Sunset vs Luxury (Daytime) Sail

Two options, both 60 minutes:

  • Luxury (Daytime) Sail — ₩60,000 adult / ₩40,000 child. Four departures from 11am. Best for clearer views of the basalt columns and Elephant Rock in full light.
  • Sunset Sail — ₩70,000 adult / ₩40,000 child. One 6pm departure (times shift by season). 360-degree golden hour panorama, once a day only.

For a first-time visitor doing a spring trip, the sunset is the one to book. The light between 6 and 7pm in May does something to the volcanic rock that daytime simply can't replicate. If you're travelling with business colleagues or planning a Jeju corporate yacht outing, the daytime sail suits group photos and conversation better — Grande Bleu also handles MICE group bookings with discount tiers.

Children under 36 months sail free. No upper age limit.

Getting to Daepo Port

Daepo Port sits on Seogwipo's southern coast, roughly 40 minutes by car from Jeju International Airport and 10 minutes from the main Jungmun resort cluster (which is where the Shilla and Parnas sit). If you're staying at either, the concierge can arrange a car — it's a short, scenic drive.

Honest Verdict

The Grande Bleu isn't trying to be a superyacht charter, and it doesn't need to. It's a well-run, unhurried hour on the water with proper safety credentials, genuinely free food and drink, and one of Korea's more photogenic coastlines as the backdrop. For a couple looking for understated luxury on a first Jeju trip — or a quiet reset between business meetings — it lands the brief.
