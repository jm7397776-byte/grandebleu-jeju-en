Why This Beats the Usual Jeju Boat Tours

Most Jeju boat tours are either overpriced ferry rides or cramped speedboats that dump you back at the dock in 30 minutes. Grande Bleu is different because it's a proper twin-hull sailing catamaran — the only certified one in South Korea — with a licensed captain and engineer onboard.

It also partners with the Shilla Hotel and Parnas Jeju, which is why you'll often see 5-star hotel guests on the same boat as backpackers. Same price, same drinks, same sunset.

For anyone doing Jeju on a budget, the value math is straightforward: one hour on the water, unlimited drinks and food, plus a fishing session — for less than a decent dinner in Seogwipo.

The 60-Minute Coastal Route (What You Actually See)

The route hugs the Seogwipo coast west of Daepo Port, and you'll see cliffs and rock formations you simply can't reach by land:

  • Elephant Rock (Kkokkiribawi) — a natural cliff shaped like an elephant drinking from the sea
  • Jingotnae Sea Canyon — dramatic vertical cliff walls
  • Wolpyeong Basalt Columns — hexagonal volcanic sea cliffs (part of Jeju's UNESCO Global Geopark)
  • Wolpyeong Sea Cave — a moody sea cave with waves crashing inside
  • Pangpaengideok — a wide flat reef plateau that's the most photogenic stop
  • Keunbada Donggul — the biggest sea cave along the coast

The catamaran does a slow 360-degree spin at the golden hour viewpoint — only once per day, only on the sunset run.

For a group of friends splitting the day, this alone justifies the ticket. Two beers on land in Seogwipo will run you more than the fishing gear rental savings here.

Fishing Onboard — Bring Nothing, Catch Something

"Will I actually catch anything?" is the most common question, and the honest answer is: usually, yes. The crew hands out rods and bait for free, and the waters off Seogwipo are known for calm conditions and reliable fish.

You might pull up:

  • Rockfish (우럭)
  • Scorpionfish (쏨뱅이)
  • Filefish (쥐치)
  • Puffer (복어) — released, obviously

Total beginners are fine. Kids too. Catch-and-release is the default if you'd rather not deal with a fish.

Sunset vs Daytime — Which One to Pick

Daytime (Luxury) tour runs four times a day starting at 11am. Better light for fishing, better visibility for the sea caves, and cheaper. Adult ₩60,000 / Child ₩40,000.

Sunset tour departs around 5:40pm (shifts with the season — confirm when you book). This is the one people fly to Jeju for. The sky slides from gold to orange to deep pink over about 20 minutes, and the catamaran does its slow rotation right in the middle of it. Adult ₩70,000 / Child ₩40,000, dropping to ₩56,000 / ₩28,000 with promotions.

If you're on a backpacker budget and traveling with friends in a group of 11+, book the sunset run and split a group discount. It's the closest Jeju gets to a Greek islands moment without Santorini prices.

Jeju in June, July, August — Is It Worth It in Summer?

Summer is peak season for good reason. Water is warm, seas are calm most afternoons, and Seogwipo's south-coast bay is one of the mellowest stretches in Korea. The sunset window stretches later — sometimes past 7:30pm in July — so you get more usable golden-hour time on the water.

The catch: afternoons can get muggy on land. An hour offshore with sea breeze and cold draft beer is genuinely one of the better ways to kill the heat.

Getting to Daepo Port

Daepo Port sits about 15 minutes by car from Jungmun Tourist Complex and 25 minutes from Seogwipo city center. Buses 202 and 520 stop nearby. Rideshare (Kakao T) works fine from anywhere in south Jeju.

Arrive 20 minutes before departure for check-in. Bring a light jacket even in summer — it gets breezy on deck once you're moving.
