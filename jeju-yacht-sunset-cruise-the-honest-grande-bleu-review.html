Pairing Seongsan Ilchulbong with Seogwipo Coastal Waters

Starting your morning at Seongsan Ilchulbong (Sunrise Peak) on the eastern side of Jeju gives you a classic introduction to local geology. Climbing the roughly 500 stone stairs requires a moderate effort, but the panoramic views of the volcanic crater and the surrounding ocean from the summit make the climb worthwhile.

By afternoon, driving down toward Seogwipo allows you to transition from rugged hiking trails to the calmer waters of the southern coast. This combination bridges the gap between active sightseeing and relaxing maritime exploration, fitting neatly into a high-end travel schedule.

What to Expect on the 60-Minute Jeju Sailing Route

Departing from Daepo Port in Seogwipo, the twin-hull catamaran navigates past dramatic volcanic rock formations sculpted by centuries of tides. The captain and licensed engineer onboard guide the vessel smoothly along a carefully planned coastal route.

  • Elephant Rock (Kkokkiribawi): A natural sea cliff bearing a striking resemblance to an elephant drinking from the ocean.
  • Wolpyeong Basalt Columns (Jusangjeolli): Hexagonal rock pillars formed by cooling lava, showcasing Jeju's UNESCO-recognized geological heritage.
  • Jingotnae Sea Canyon & Wolpyeong Sea Cave: Deep vertical rock clefts and mysterious caves where ocean waves crash against ancient basalt.
  • Pangpaengideok & Keunbada Donggul: Broad flat rock plateaus and the largest coastal sea cave on this stretch of shoreline.

Unlimited Onboard Amenities and Fishing Experience

One of the standout features of this cruise is the complimentary food and beverage service available to all passengers throughout the journey. Guests can enjoy freshly poured Korean draft beer, red and white wine, local tangerine juice, bottled water, Jeju specialty cookies, and traditional instant Korean ramyeon cooked fresh onboard.

The tour also includes a casual, beginner-friendly fishing experience where the crew supplies all necessary rods and bait. Visitors frequently catch local species such as rockfish (우럭), scorpionfish (쏨뱅이), filefish (쥐치), and pufferfish (복어), with a strict catch-and-release policy to protect the local marine ecosystem.
