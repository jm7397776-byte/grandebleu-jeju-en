Why a Catamaran, Not a Regular Yacht

Most "yacht tours" in Asia run on single-hull boats that pitch hard the moment the swell picks up. A catamaran has two hulls, so it sits flatter on the water. If you get seasick easily, this matters more than any view.

Grande Bleu has been operating out of Daepo Port since 2011 and has served over 100,000 guests. It partners with Shilla Hotel and Parnas Jeju, has a licensed captain and engineer onboard, and holds the top safety grade in Korea — the only catamaran here allowed to board guests without life vests. The 60-Minute Coastal Route

The route runs along Seogwipo's southern cliffs — a stretch that's genuinely hard to see any other way. Highlights:

  • Elephant Rock (Kkokkiribawi) — a cliff that actually looks like an elephant
  • Wolpyeong Basalt Columns — hexagonal volcanic sea cliffs, part of Jeju's UNESCO Global Geopark
  • Wolpyeong Sea Cave — waves crashing into a dark hollow
  • Jingotnae Sea Canyon — vertical rock walls dropping into the water
  • Pangpaengideok — a flat reef plateau at sea level, the most photogenic stop
  • Large Sea Cave (Keunbada Donggul) — the biggest one on this coast

If you've compared Jeju against Bali or the Greek islands, the terrain here is different — black volcanic rock, colder blue water, fewer boats around you. In winter the light goes gold much earlier, which means better photos without staying out late.

Wild Dolphins (If You're Lucky)

This stretch of coast is a known habitat for Jeju's indigenous bottlenose dolphins. Sightings aren't guaranteed — they're wild animals, not scheduled entertainment — but they happen often enough that crew keep an eye out. Winter sightings still occur. Treat it as a bonus, not a promise.

Fishing Without the Faff

Fishing is included on every tour. Rods, bait, and one-on-one help from crew — all free. You'll likely pull up rockfish, scorpionfish, filefish, or puffer, all local Jeju species. Catch-and-release is fine if you'd rather not deal with the fish.

For a solo traveller in your 20s this is the part that unexpectedly wins people over. You're not committing to a fishing trip, you're just holding a rod for 15 minutes with a beer in your other hand.

Sunset vs Luxury (Daytime) Tour

  • Sunset Tour (₩70,000): Departs around sunset — currently 6:40pm, but shifts with the season. Golden hour, 360° panoramic light, best for photos. Confirm exact departure when you book.
  • Luxury Tour (₩60,000): Runs up to 5 times a day from 11am. Cheaper, better if you're bringing kids who won't last till evening, or want to fit it into a packed day.

For winter specifically, sunset falls earlier (around 5:30–6pm in December–January), so you're not out late. Ideal for a family dinner afterwards in Seogwipo.
