Why a Catamaran Beats a Regular Jeju Tour

Most Jeju itineraries stack the same stops: Seongsan Ilchulbong at sunrise, Hallasan hike, Hyeopjae Beach. All good, all crowded, all on land.

The south coast between Seogwipo's cliffs is different. You can't actually see most of it from shore — the sea caves, basalt columns and the wide reef plateaus only reveal themselves from the water.

Grande Bleu is Korea's only certified catamaran brand, which matters for two reasons. Twin hulls mean far less rocking than a monohull yacht (good news if you or your friends aren't sea-legs types), and the wide deck means everyone gets a seat with a view.

For travellers in their 60s and up, that stability is the difference between a relaxed hour and a queasy one. The boarding ramp is flat, the deck has railings, and there's shaded indoor seating if the summer sun gets fierce.

The 60-Minute Coastal Route

The route hugs Jeju's southern volcanic coastline, which UNESCO recognises as part of its Global Geopark network. In one loop you pass:

  • Elephant Rock (Kkokkiribawi) — a cliff shaped exactly like an elephant drinking from the sea
  • Jingotnae Sea Canyon — dramatic vertical cliff walls plunging into deep water
  • Wolpyeong Basalt Columns — hexagonal volcanic pillars, the same geology that made the Giant's Causeway famous
  • Wolpyeong Sea Cave and the Large Sea Cave (Keunbada Donggul)
  • Pangpaengideok — a broad flat reef platform that's genuinely photogenic without being touristy

The captain slows down at each landmark for photos, and the crew narrates in Korean with English cues on request.

What's Actually Free (No Catch)

This is the part that surprises most first-time guests. Everything on the drinks and snacks menu is genuinely unlimited — no per-glass charge, no upsell:

  • Draft beer (Korean fresh brew, poured onboard)
  • Red and white wine
  • Fresh-squeezed Jeju tangerine juice (worth trying — Jeju's citrus is a local specialty)
  • Bottled water
  • Local cookies and traditional Jeju snacks
  • Hot Korean ramyeon, cooked to order on the boat

For a premium tour at ₩60,000–₩70,000, the food and drinks alone would cost roughly the same in a Seogwipo bar. That shifts the value calculation considerably.

Fishing Included — Real Jeju Species

Every cruise includes a fishing segment with rods, bait and coaching from the crew. It's beginner-friendly and you're targeting genuine local species:

  • Rockfish (우럭)
  • Scorpionfish (쏨뱅이)
  • Filefish (쥐치)
  • Occasional puffer (복어)

Catch-and-release is standard, and it's a nice equaliser — grandparents, kids and reluctant teens all end up leaning over the rail together. This is one of the few Jeju family activities that actually engages three generations at once.

Summer (June–August): What Nobody Tells You

Jeju's peak summer is humid and hot inland, but the sea breeze on the water knocks 5–7°C off the temperature. That's why late-afternoon sailings from Daepo Port book out fastest in July and August.

A few practical notes for Jeju June July August travel:

  • Sunset shifts from around 7:40pm in late June to 7:10pm by late August — check your departure time
  • Bring a light jacket; the wind picks up once you leave the harbour
  • Sunscreen and sunglasses are essential even at golden hour
  • Afternoon showers are common but usually pass in 20 minutes

Is It Worth the Price? An Honest Take

At ₩70,000 for the Sunset Tour, Grande Bleu sits at the premium end of Jeju sailing options. What you get in return:

  • A 60-minute sail on Korea's only certified catamaran
  • Access to coastline you literally cannot see any other way
  • Unlimited food and drinks that easily cover ₩25,000–₩30,000 of the ticket

Compared to a Maldives sunset dhoni (often USD $80+) or a Santorini catamaran (€90+), the Jeju option is priced fairly for what's included. Klook reviews consistently sit above 4.7/5 across thousands of ratings.

Is it "life-changing"? No. Is it a solid, well-run hour that gives you a different angle on Jeju? Yes.

Where does the Jeju yacht depart from? All Grande Bleu cruises depart from Daepo Port in Seogwipo, on Jeju Island's southern coast (GPS 33.2287°N 126.4318°E). It's about a 40-minute drive from Jeju International Airport.
