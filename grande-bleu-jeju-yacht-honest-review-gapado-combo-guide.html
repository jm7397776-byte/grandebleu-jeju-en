Why Pair Gapado With a Jeju Yacht Tour

Most first-timers do Gapado as a standalone half-day. Locals and repeat visitors know it pairs neatly with a Seogwipo yacht run in the late afternoon — you get the slow island walk in the morning, then see Jeju from the water at sunset.

Gapado is Korea's southernmost inhabited island, reached by a short ferry from Moseulpo Port. The ferry runs limited times daily, so check the return schedule the moment you land. No cars on the island — you walk or rent an e-scooter.

The Olle Trail 10-1 loops the coast for about 4km, past basalt walls, haenyeo (female diver) work sheds, and the southernmost point marker. On clear days you can spot Marado lighthouse across the water. It's flat, quiet, and takes 1.5–2 hours at a wander.

What Gapado is good for: peaceful walks, photography, seeing haenyeo culture without the staged tourist version. What it's not: nightlife, restaurants, or shopping. Bring what you need from Moseulpo.

By 3–4pm you can ferry back and drive 30 minutes to Daepo Port for the sunset cruise. That's the sequence I'd recommend for solo travellers in their 40s doing a friends' trip — low-effort, high-payoff, and you don't repeat the same coastline twice.

The Yacht Itself — What You Actually Get

Grande Bleu is a proper twin-hull catamaran, not a repurposed fishing boat. It's the only vessel in Jeju rated at the highest safety tier — you don't need to wear a life jacket on deck, which tells you something about the certification. Licensed captain and engineer onboard, AED on the vessel, blankets and seasickness meds available on request.

The 60-minute route covers a genuinely good stretch of the Seogwipo coast:

  • Elephant Rock (Kkokkiribawi) — natural cliff shaped like an elephant's head
  • Wolpyeong Basalt Columns — hexagonal volcanic sea cliffs, part of Jeju's UNESCO Global Geopark
  • Wolpyeong Sea Cave and the larger Keunbada Donggul
  • Jingotnae Sea Canyon — dramatic vertical cliff walls
  • Pangpaengideok — a wide flat reef plateau, weirdly photogenic at golden hour

The route also crosses the habitat of Jeju's resident bottlenose dolphins. No promises — they're wild — but sightings do happen.

Jeju vs Bali vs the Greek Islands (Quick Take)

Bali gives you variety and cheap drinks but crowded boats. Greek island sailing is stunning but pricey and needs a longer trip. Jeju sits in the middle: 60 minutes, mid-tier price, volcanic geology you won't see elsewhere, and — crucially for the peaceful, healing angle — the boat isn't packed to the rails. Capacity is 91 but it rarely feels full.

If you're a Korea resident already, you don't need to fly anywhere. If you're visiting from Singapore, Hong Kong, or elsewhere in SEA, Jeju flights are shorter and cheaper than Bali from most hubs.
