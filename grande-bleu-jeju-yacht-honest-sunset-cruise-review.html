Why a Catamaran Beats a Regular Boat

Most Jeju boat tours run on single-hull vessels that lean and rock. A catamaran has two hulls, which means less sway and a wide, flat deck you can actually walk around on with a wine glass in hand.

For couples in their 60s or anyone who isn't sold on seasickness as a holiday feature, this matters. Grande Bleu is the only certified catamaran brand in Korea, and the deck space between the twin hulls is where you'll want to be when the sky changes colour.

The company also partners with the Shilla Hotel and Parnas Jeju — a small detail, but a useful trust signal if you're the type who checks who a business works with before booking.

What a Jeju Spring Sunset Actually Looks Like

Spring (March through May) is arguably the smartest window for a Jeju sailing trip. The air is mild, humidity hasn't kicked in yet, and the sea is calm enough that the boat glides rather than bounces.

Sunset departure shifts with the season, so confirm your time when booking. In May, expect roughly a 6pm push-off from Daepo Port.

As the catamaran heads toward the Wolpyeong Basalt Columns, the sky moves through orange, pink, and deep violet — and because you're mid-sea, you get the full 360-degree version rather than a slice framed by buildings. It happens once a day. That's the pitch, and it's a fair one.

The 60-Minute Coastal Route

The route hugs some of the most photogenic stretches of Jeju's southern coast — the kind of geology you can't reach on foot.

  • Elephant Rock (Kkokkiribawi) — a cliff shaped exactly like it sounds
  • Jingotnae Sea Canyon — dramatic vertical cliff walls
  • Wolpyeong Basalt Columns — hexagonal volcanic sea cliffs, part of Jeju's UNESCO Global Geopark
  • Wolpyeong Sea Cave — waves crashing into a hidden hollow
  • Pangpaengideok — a wide, flat reef plateau at sea level
  • Large Sea Cave (Keunbada Donggul) — the biggest cave on this stretch of coast

Seeing these from the water is genuinely different from the clifftop viewpoints most day-tour buses stop at. Same Jeju, different angle.

Fishing, Included

Every tour includes a fishing session with real Jeju species — rockfish, scorpionfish, filefish, and puffer. Rods and bait are provided free, staff will help absolute beginners, and catch-and-release is fine if you'd rather not deal with a fish.

It's a nice five-minute story to take home, and a genuine reason this works as a Jeju corporate yacht outing or MICE booking. A group discount is available for team building trips.

Is It Worth It for a Couple in Their 60s?

Honestly — yes, with caveats. The deck is flat and stable, boarding is straightforward at Daepo Port, and 60 minutes is short enough that you're not committed to a whole evening if the weather turns.

The catamaran is less physically demanding than climbing Seongsan Ilchulbong or walking the Songaksan coastal path, which makes it a sensible pairing with a slower Seogwipo-focused itinerary.

About the Weather (Don't Cancel Too Early)

A common instinct is to cancel the moment the forecast shows rain. Don't.

If conditions are genuinely unsafe, Grande Bleu contacts you first to reschedule or refund. Light rain often clears by the 6pm slot, and overcast skies can produce better sunset photos than a cloudless one. Check with the operator before making the call.
