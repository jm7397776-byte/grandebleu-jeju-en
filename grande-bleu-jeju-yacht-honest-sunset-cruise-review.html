Is It Actually Worth the Price?

Short answer for the honeymooning retirees: yes, if you go at sunset. At around ₩70,000 a head it sits comfortably in the Jeju budget travel bracket for a couples experience — cheaper than a decent seafood dinner for two in Seogwipo, and you get free-flowing drinks thrown in.

Compared to sunset sails in Santorini (upwards of €90pp) or Bali gulet trips (Rp 1.5m+), Jeju is quietly the better-value catamaran outing in Asia. It's also one of the few Jeju summer activities that keeps you cool without demanding you hike up a volcano in 30°C heat.

The catamaran design matters more than it sounds. Twin hulls mean almost no roll — good news if you or your partner have never been on a boat before, or if your sea legs aren't what they were.

What You Actually Do for 60 Minutes

The route hugs Jeju's south coast, which is the island's dramatic side — cliffs, sea caves, hexagonal basalt columns. It's UNESCO-grade geology without the hike.

Highlights on the loop:

  • Elephant Rock — the trunk-shaped cliff that gives the coast its nickname
  • Jingotnae Sea Canyon — sheer vertical walls dropping into deep water
  • Wolpyeong Basalt Columns — the hexagonal sea cliffs, part of Jeju's UNESCO Geopark
  • Wolpyeong Sea Cave and the Large Sea Cave — best photographed with waves crashing in
  • Pangpaengideok — a wide flat reef plateau that looks unreal at golden hour
  • 360-degree panoramic sunset — the whole reason you booked

You'll also get a proper fishing go on the deck. Rods, bait and instruction included, aimed at Jeju local species — rockfish, scorpionfish, filefish, the occasional puffer. Catch-and-release if you'd rather not deal with a bucket of fish on your honeymoon.

Why Sunset, Specifically

Jeju's south coast faces the right way for golden hour, and Seogwipo cliffs light up copper-orange for about twenty minutes before the sun drops. Grande Bleu times the loop so you're at the widest sea view — Pangpaengideok reef — when it happens.

Morning and midday Jeju sailing trips exist and are cheaper (Luxury Tour ₩60,000), but for a couple, the sunset slot is the one worth paying the extra ₩10,000 for. Once a day only, so it books out first in Jeju June, July, August peak season.

Summer sunset in Jeju falls roughly between 19:30 and 19:50 — check the exact departure when you book, and aim to arrive at Daepo Port 30 minutes early.

Getting There: Daepo Port

Daepo Port sits on the south coast, about 15 minutes by taxi from central Seogwipo and roughly an hour from Jeju International Airport. If you're already staying in the Jungmun resort area (Shilla, Lotte, Parnas), it's a 10-minute cab.

For a couple travelling light and on a backpacker budget, the 600-series airport bus down to Seogwipo plus a short taxi is the cheapest route in.

What Nobody Tells You

A few honest pointers picked up from 100,000+ guests:

  • Wear flat shoes. Deck is stable but not the place for heels.
  • Bring a light jacket even in August — sea breeze bites after sundown.
  • Sunglasses matter more than a camera. The horizon glare is real.
  • Phones take fine sunset photos here; don't stress the gear.
  • Seniors and non-swimmers are welcome — the catamaran barely rocks, life jackets provided.
