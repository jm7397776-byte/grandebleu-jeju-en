What to Expect on the 60-Minute Sunset Cruise

The star attraction is, of course, the sunset. Departing from Daepo Port in Seogwipo, the 60-minute route takes you along the stunning coastline, showcasing geological wonders that feel almost otherworldly. You'll glide past the dramatic Elephant Rock (Kkokkiribawi), its trunk-like formation a perfect photo op. Then, there's the deep Jingotnae Sea Canyon, with its sheer, vertical walls plunging into the ocean.

The highlight for geology buffs will be the Wolpyeong Basalt Columns (Wolpyeong Jusangjeolli). These hexagonal volcanic sea cliffs, reminiscent of UNESCO World Heritage sites, are a testament to Jeju's fiery origins. You'll also see the mysterious Wolpyeong Sea Cave, where waves crash dramatically, and the photogenic Pangpaengideok, a broad, flat reef plateau. The sheer scale of the Large Sea Cave (Keunbada Donggul) is also impressive.

The real magic happens during the golden hour. You’re treated to 360-degree panoramic views as the sun dips below the horizon, casting a warm, ethereal glow over the ocean. This is the slow side of K-culture – a moment of quiet appreciation for nature's spectacle, far removed from the bustling cityscapes. It’s the kind of scene that makes you pause and reflect, a welcome contrast to a packed business trip.

What About Fishing? An Unexpected Bonus

Yes, fishing is included on all tours, which adds another layer to the "things to do in Jeju" list. You’ll have the chance to try your hand at catching local Jeju species like rockfish, scorpionfish, filefish, and puffer. Fishing rods and bait are provided free of charge, making it accessible even if you've never fished before. It’s a relaxed, beginner-friendly activity, and there’s even a catch-and-release option if you prefer. This unexpected inclusion adds a touch of local flavour and engagement that many guests appreciate.

Autumn in Jeju: What to Bring and What to Know

Visiting in October (Jeju in month 10) means you're in for pleasant autumn weather. The air is cool and refreshing, perfect for being out on the water. While the sun can still be warm, evenings on the sea can get chilly. We recommend bringing a light jacket or shawl. Comfortable shoes are a must, as you'll be walking on a boat. Don't forget your camera – the photographic opportunities are abundant, from the coastal geology to the dramatic sunset.

The sea conditions in autumn are generally favourable. However, as with any sea voyage, there can be slight rocking. The catamaran design, with its twin hulls, significantly reduces roll compared to monohull boats, making it a more stable platform. Nevertheless, if you’re particularly prone to motion sickness, it’s always wise to be prepared with medication.
