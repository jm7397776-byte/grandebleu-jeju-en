Why a Catamaran in Jeju, Not Bali

Fair question. Bali has villas. The Maldives has overwater bungalows. Jeju's pitch is different: a UNESCO Global Geopark coastline of basalt columns and sea caves, sailed on the only certified twin-hull catamaran in South Korea — with a licensed captain and engineer onboard.

The catamaran design matters. Twin hulls mean far less rocking than a monohull yacht, which is why Grande Bleu holds Jeju's highest safety rating — the only vessel here you can legally board without a life vest. For solo travellers in their 50s who'd rather sip a glass of wine than clutch a railing, that's the whole point.

Partnerships with Shilla Hotel and Parnas Jeju give you a fair sense of the standard.

The 60-Minute Route (What You Actually See)

The coastline west of Daepo Port is the good stuff — quieter than the eastern beaches, and only visible properly from the water.

  • Wolpyeong Basalt Columns — hexagonal volcanic cliffs, part of Jeju's UNESCO Geopark
  • Elephant Rock (Kkokkiribawi) — a natural cliff shaped, unmistakably, like an elephant
  • Jingotnae Sea Canyon — dramatic vertical walls dropping straight into the sea
  • Wolpyeong Sea Cave & Large Sea Cave — two sea caves the shore path can't reach
  • Pangpaengideok — a wide, flat reef plateau that photographs beautifully in golden hour

You'll also pass through a known habitat of wild Indo-Pacific bottlenose dolphins. No promises — they're wild — but sightings are frequent enough that the crew watches for them on every sailing.

Spring (March–May): The Quiet Season Nobody Talks About

Jeju's peak season is summer. Spring is when the island is at its best for adults travelling alone or on business: 15–20°C, clear light, canola fields on shore, and half the crowds.

May sunsets from the catamaran fall around 7:15–7:30pm, so the 6:40pm sunset sailing lands you in open water right as the light turns. Sunset departure times shift monthly — confirm when you book.

For a business traveller with a free afternoon, the appeal is obvious: a 60-minute window that produces the trip's best photographs, no itinerary-building required.
