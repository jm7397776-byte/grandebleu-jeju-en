Why a Catamaran (and Not Just Any Boat)

Most "yacht" listings in Korea are monohulls that lean when they turn. A catamaran sits on two hulls, so the deck stays flat. For anyone in their 60s, or friends who'd rather sip wine than clutch a railing, that's the practical difference.

Grande Bleu is the only certified catamaran brand in South Korea, running since 2011 with over 100,000 guests served. It also holds partnerships with Shilla Hotel and Parnas Jeju — a decent trust signal if you're weighing operators.

The Sunset Tour runs once a day, timed to the golden hour. Summer light in Jeju between June and August stretches long, and the horizon out of Seogwipo faces open water — you get the full 360-degree wash of colour without land blocking the view.

What You'll Actually See

The one-hour route hugs the southern coast, and the geology does most of the work:

  • Elephant Rock (Kkokkiribawi) — a cliff shaped, unmistakably, like an elephant drinking
  • Wolpyeong Basalt Columns — hexagonal volcanic pillars, the same UNESCO-grade geology that put Jeju on the geopark map
  • Wolpyeong Sea Cave — waves crash straight into a cave mouth you can only reach by boat
  • Jingotnae Sea Canyon — vertical cliff walls, dramatic in evening light
  • Pangpaengideok — a wide flat reef that catches the last sun
  • Large Sea Cave (Keunbada Donggul) — the biggest on this coastline

None of this is visible from the road. That's the point of doing a Jeju sailing trip rather than another coastal drive.

For a couple travelling with friends, that's a full sunset happy hour built into the ticket. Compare it to a bar bill at any hotel rooftop in Seogwipo and the ₩70,000 sunset fare stops looking premium.

Blankets are stocked for the deck. Summer evenings in Jeju can still feel breezy on open water, especially after sunset.

Fishing — Yes, Really

Every tour includes rod and bait, free. You can drop a line for local Jeju species: rockfish, scorpionfish, filefish, puffer. Catch-and-release is fine.

It's beginner-friendly, and honestly a good icebreaker on a friends trip when someone's had one glass of wine too many and decides they're a fisherman now.

Is It Worth It for a Premium Friends Trip?

Short version: yes, if you value time over money and want a Jeju activity that doesn't involve walking.

For travellers in their 60s, this is one of the least strenuous scenic experiences on the island — you board a flat deck, sit down, and the coast comes to you. Compared to hiking Hallasan or clambering around Manjanggul lava tube, the effort-to-reward ratio is unmatched.

Compared to a sunset sail in Bali or the Greek islands, Jeju's version is shorter (60 minutes vs 2–3 hours elsewhere), which for many older travellers is a feature, not a bug. You get the light, the food, the photos, and you're back on land before dinner.

Best Time: Summer Sunset Cruise in Jeju (June–August)

June through August gives you the latest sunsets of the year — the sky doesn't fully go until nearly 8 PM in mid-summer. That means the 5:40 PM departure catches full daylight, transitions through golden hour, and lands you back at Daepo Port around dusk.

Sea conditions in summer are generally calm on the south coast. Bring a light layer, a hat that won't blow off, and slip-on shoes.
