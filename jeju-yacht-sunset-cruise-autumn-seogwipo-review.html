Autumn Walking on Olle 6 Meets the Open Sea

Olle Route 6 stretches 11 kilometers from Soesokkak Estuary right into downtown Seogwipo. In autumn, silver grass borders the coastal path while crisp air keeps the walking comfortable. The gentle trails around Sojeongbang Waterfall and the high sea cliffs near Jeongbang Waterfall give you sweeping views of Seopseom and Munseom islands.

However, viewing these volcanic formations from shore only shows half the picture. Wrapping up a morning hike and heading to Daepo Port for a 60-minute cruise lets you see those same cliffs from the open water. Because twin hulls balance a catamaran on both sides, stability is high and seasickness is rare, making it ideal for young children and older parents.

Coastal Course: The UNESCO Geopark Formations

The 60-minute sailing route passes some of the most striking geological landmarks along the southern coast of Jeju, Korea. This coastal stretch forms part of the Jeju UNESCO Global Geopark network, sculpted over millennia by basaltic lava flows.

  • Elephant Rock (Kkokkiribawi): A distinct natural cliff formation jutting into the surf that resembles an elephant drinking from the ocean.
  • Jingotnae Sea Canyon: A narrow volcanic inlet featuring dramatic, vertical rock faces cut deep into the coastline.
  • Wolpyeong Basalt Columns (Wolpyeong Jusangjeolli): Towering hexagonal volcanic pillars rising directly out of the water.
  • Wolpyeong Sea Cave: A hollowed-out sea cavern carved by relentless southern ocean swells.
  • Pangpaengideok: A wide, flat basalt reef plateau sitting right at sea level.
  • Large Sea Cave (Keunbada Donggul): The largest natural sea cavern along this section of the coast.

During the late afternoon golden hour, the low autumn sun strikes these dark cliffs, offering sweeping 360-degree views across the southern horizon. Indo-Pacific bottlenose dolphins also frequently surface near these waters.
