Why This One Over the Other Jeju Boat Tours

Jeju has plenty of boat operators, but most run mono-hull fishing boats that pitch heavily in the swell. Grande Bleu is the only certified catamaran brand in Korea — twin hulls, wider deck, noticeably more stable. If your partner gets seasick, this matters.

The other trust signal: Shilla Hotel and Parnas Jeju send their guests here. Five-star hotels don't attach their name to anything that might embarrass them.

Compared to a sunset sail in Santorini or Phuket, prices are lower and the coastline is arguably more dramatic — think UNESCO-grade basalt columns rather than whitewashed villages.

The 60-Minute Route

Departing Daepo Port, the boat traces a compact coastal loop past some of Seogwipo's best geology:

  • Wolpyeong Basalt Columns — hexagonal volcanic sea cliffs (part of Jeju's UNESCO Global Geopark)
  • Wolpyeong Sea Cave and the Large Sea Cave (Keunbada Donggul)
  • Elephant Rock — a natural cliff that genuinely looks like an elephant
  • Jingotnae Sea Canyon — vertical cliff walls dropping straight into the sea
  • Pangpaengideok — a wide flat reef plateau, the most photogenic stop
  • Wild waters of Jeju's southern bottlenose dolphin population (roughly 120 residents along the Seogwipo–Daejeong coast)

Dolphins aren't guaranteed — they're wild — but the boat's route passes directly through their feeding grounds. When they show up, they surf the wake beside the hull.

Sunset vs Luxury (Day) Tour — Which One?

For couples and honeymooners: the Sunset Tour, no contest. Spring departures in Jeju sit around 5:40 pm (times shift with the season, so confirm at booking). Watching Hallasan's silhouette darken against a pink sky, glass of wine in hand, is the kind of quiet moment you actually remember from a trip.

For families or first-timers: the Luxury Tour runs four times daily from 11 am. Better light for photos of the basalt columns, and kids get to see the rock formations properly.

Both tours include the same route, food, and fishing.

Fishing on the Deck

Rods, bait, and crew guidance are included. Local catch typically includes rockfish, scorpionfish, filefish, and puffer. Beginner-friendly — if you've never held a rod, the crew sets you up. Catch-and-release is the norm.

Best Time to Sail: Spring (March–May)

Spring in Jeju gives you calm seas, mild temperatures (15–20°C), cherry blossoms onshore, and clearer horizons than the humid summer months. May in particular is a sweet spot for a Jeju honeymoon — low rainfall, long golden hour, and the shoulder-season pricing hasn't kicked into peak yet.

Bring: a light jacket (it's cooler on the water), sunglasses, and a phone lanyard if you plan to shoot from the bow.
