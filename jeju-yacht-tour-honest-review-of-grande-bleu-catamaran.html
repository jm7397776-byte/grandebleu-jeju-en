Why a Catamaran, and Why This One

Most "yacht tours" in Asia are monohulls — narrow, tilty, and if the sea's choppy, someone's going green by minute 20. Grande Bleu runs Korea's only certified catamaran (twin-hull), which basically means the deck stays flat. That matters if you're bringing kids, grandparents, or your camera.

It's not a hidden gem in the sense that no one knows about it — over 100,000 guests have been through since 2011, and the Shilla and Parnas Jeju both funnel their guests here. But it is underrated internationally. Most Hong Kong itineraries skip Seogwipo's south coast entirely, which is a mistake. Learn more on the About Grande Bleu page.

The 60-Minute Coastal Route

The route hugs Jeju's south coast, which is quietly one of the most dramatic stretches of volcanic shoreline in Korea. You're not just floating around a marina — you actually cover ground:

  • Elephant Rock (Kkokkiribawi) — a cliff genuinely shaped like an elephant, not the usual "if you squint" tourist rock
  • Jingotnae Sea Canyon — vertical cliff walls dropping straight into the water
  • Wolpyeong Basalt Columns — hexagonal volcanic sea cliffs, part of Jeju's UNESCO Global Geopark geology
  • Wolpyeong Sea Cave — waves crashing into a cave you can only see from the water
  • Pangpaengideok — a wide, flat reef plateau that photographs ridiculously well in golden hour
  • Large Sea Cave (Keunbada Donggul) — the biggest sea cave on this coast

You cannot see any of this from land. That's the honest ROI argument for the ticket.

The ramyeon at sea, watching cliffs go past — that's the slow side of K-culture that doesn't make it into K-dramas but probably should.

Fishing, Included

You get real rods and bait, free, and you're fishing for actual Jeju species — rockfish (우럭), scorpionfish (쏨뱅이), filefish (쥐치), sometimes puffer (복어). Total beginner setup. Catch-and-release is fine if you'd rather not deal with a fish.

For a solo traveller it's a low-key icebreaker; for families it's the part the kids will remember over dinner three months later.

Spring (Mar–May): Why the Timing Works

May in Jeju is the sweet spot before summer humidity lands. Sea's calm, air's clear, and the golden hour light on the basalt columns is the kind of thing you don't need a filter for. Spring is also low-key shoulder season for international visitors, so the boat isn't packed even at premium tier.

If you're doing a family vacation and comparing Jeju sailing to, say, a Maldives sunset cruise — the price gap is enormous. A comparable Maldives catamaran tour runs 4–6× what Grande Bleu charges, and you don't get the volcanic coastline.
