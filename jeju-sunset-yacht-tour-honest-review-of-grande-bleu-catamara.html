The 60-Minute Seogwipo Coastal Route

Departing from Daepo Port in Seogwipo Jeju, the vessel heads out along some of the most striking volcanic coastlines in South Korea. The 60-minute route takes you past geological landmarks formed thousands of years ago by cooling lava. You will sail past Elephant Rock, a natural sea cliff shaped remarkably like an elephant drinking from the ocean.

Next, the route passes the Wolpyeong Basalt Columns, hexagonal volcanic sea cliffs similar to columns found in UNESCO-style geoparks. You will also navigate past the Wolpyeong Sea Cave, Pangpaengideok (a wide flat reef plateau), and the deep vertical walls of Jingotnae Sea Canyon. If you are lucky, the boat may pass through known habitats for wild Indo-Pacific bottlenose dolphins, though wildlife sightings depend entirely on nature.

What's Included: Free Unlimited Food and Drinks

One of the standout features of this Jeju activity is the onboard hospitality. Once the catamaran reaches open water, the crew opens a floating snack bar with unlimited options at no extra charge. Guests can enjoy fresh draft beer, red and white wine, Jeju tangerine juice, and bottled water.

For comfort during the autumn breeze, the crew cooks traditional Korean ramyeon onboard, accompanied by local Jeju snacks and cookies. Everything is completely self-serve and included in the ticket price. There are no hidden fees or upcharges for drinks once you step on deck.

Onboard Fishing and Twin-Hull Stability

Catamarans use two parallel hulls connected by a wide deck, which significantly reduces swaying compared to single-hull motorboats. This design makes the Jeju catamaran experience much more stable for travelers prone to motion sickness. Grande Bleu operates licensed vessels with a certified captain and engineer onboard.

Midway through the excursion, the crew sets up a casual fishing experience. All rods, reels, and bait are provided free of charge, requiring no preparation from visitors. You can try catching local species like rockfish (우럭), scorpionfish (쏨뱅이), filefish (쥐치), or pufferfish (복어). Following local practice, fish caught during the trip are typically released back into the sea, making it a sustainable and fun activity for all ages.
