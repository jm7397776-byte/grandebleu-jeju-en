Why a Catamaran (and Not Just Another Boat)

Grande Bleu runs Korea's only certified catamaran fleet — twin-hull sailing yachts that sit flatter on the water than a monohull. For parents travelling with young kids or older relatives, that stability matters more than it sounds. Less rocking means less seasickness, and it's the reason Grande Bleu is the only vessel in Jeju certified to operate without mandatory life-jackets — a small detail that speaks to the safety rating.

You can walk the deck freely, sit inside the cabin, or lean against the bow with a glass of wine. No shuffling, no queuing, no wet plastic vests over your linen shirt.

The Coastal Route: What You'll Actually See

The 60-minute loop hugs Seogwipo's volcanic south coast — a stretch you simply can't reach on foot. Highlights include:

  • Elephant Rock (Kkokkiribawi) — a cliff shaped, unmistakably, like an elephant drinking
  • Jingotnae Sea Canyon — vertical walls plunging straight into the sea
  • Wolpyeong Basalt Columns — hexagonal volcanic cliffs, part of Jeju's UNESCO Global Geopark
  • Wolpyeong Sea Cave and the Large Sea Cave (Keunbada Donggul) — the biggest on this coastline
  • Pangpaengideok — a wide flat reef plateau that photographs beautifully in golden hour
  • Wild bottlenose dolphins are frequent visitors; the crew slows down if a pod appears

In autumn (September to November), the light hits these basalt columns at a lower angle, deepening the black rock against the blue. It's the reason Jeju October is quietly the best month to sail here.

Fishing for Beginners (Kids Love This Part)

Rods and bait are provided free. You're fishing for real Jeju species — rockfish, scorpionfish, filefish, and the occasional puffer — in waters the crew knows by heart. Catch-and-release is standard, and beginners land something more often than you'd expect.

Parents' tip: this is the twenty-minute stretch where kids stop asking for the iPad.

Sunset vs Daytime: Which to Book

Sunset Tour (₩70,000) departs around 6:40 pm in autumn — timed so you're mid-sea when the horizon turns amber. This is the once-a-day, 360-degree golden hour window, and it's the tour most guests remember.

Luxury Daytime Tour (₩60,000) runs multiple times between 11 am and 5:20 pm. Better for photography of the basalt columns and sea caves, since the light is sharper and shadows sit deeper in the volcanic geology.

For solo travellers on a healing trip, the last sunset departure — with a glass of wine, no schedule, and the coastline sliding past — is the quiet win.
