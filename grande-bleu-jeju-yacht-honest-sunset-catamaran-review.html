Why a Catamaran, and Why This One

Most Jeju sailing options are monohulls — the kind that lean when the wind picks up and make half the boat regret their lunch. Grande Bleu runs a twin-hull catamaran, which stays flat. That matters more than it sounds. You can walk around with a glass of wine, kids can move about, and the deck is wide enough to actually sit and look at things.

It's also Korea's only certified catamaran brand, with a licensed captain and engineer on every sailing. Not a marketing line — it's the reason five-star hotels like Shilla and Parnas route their guests here.

Compared to a Bali sunset cruise or the Greek island day-boats, this is shorter (an hour), cheaper, and honestly better organised. You're not stuck on a boat for six hours with strangers.

The 60-Minute Coast: What You'll Actually See

The route hugs Seogwipo's south coast, which is part of a UNESCO Global Geopark. In order:

  • Wolpyeong Basalt Columns — hexagonal volcanic cliffs dropping into the sea
  • Wolpyeong Sea Cave — waves smashing into a genuinely creepy dark hollow
  • Elephant Rock (Kkokkiribawi) — yes, it looks like an elephant
  • Jingotnae Sea Canyon — vertical cliff walls, the kind of scale you don't get from the coastal road
  • Pangpaengideok — a flat reef plateau that's the boat's best photo spot
  • Large Sea Cave — the biggest on the coast, saved for near the end

You've seen these cliffs from the Olle trails. From the water they're a completely different animal — bigger, blacker, more geologically strange. The captain slows down at each one so you can actually take it in.

Fishing: Actually Fun, Even if You've Never Held a Rod

Rods and bait are provided. The crew shows you how to bait the hook. You drop the line over the side while the boat is anchored near the cliffs. Common catches around Seogwipo:

  • Rockfish (우럭)
  • Scorpionfish (쏨뱅이)
  • Filefish (쥐치)
  • Pufferfish (복어)

Catch-and-release is available if you'd rather send them back. This is not a "serious fishing charter" — it's fifteen minutes of low-effort fun that somehow becomes the highlight for half the guests.

The Sunset Bit

Golden hour on the Seogwipo coast, viewed from a flat catamaran deck, is one of those things Instagram doesn't quite capture. The cliffs turn orange, the sea goes glassy, and you get a full 360-degree horizon — no crowds photobombing.

If you're travelling as a couple or with close friends, this is the moment. Skip the tripod-jostle at Seongsan Ilchulbong. This is quieter and, frankly, more romantic.

Summer (June–August) Notes

Peak Jeju summer means humid land and cooler sea breeze — the yacht is genuinely the most comfortable place to be between 5 and 7pm. A few practical points:

  • Bring a light layer; even in August the wind picks up offshore
  • Sunglasses and reef-safe SPF are worth packing
  • Book 1–2 weeks ahead in July/August — Korean domestic travellers fill weekend slots first
  • The sunset tour is more popular; the earlier Luxury Tour is cheaper and less crowded
