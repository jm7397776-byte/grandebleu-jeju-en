Why a Catamaran (and Not a Regular Boat)?

Fair question, especially if you're travelling with older parents or grandkids. A catamaran has two parallel hulls instead of one, which means less roll and far less seasickness than a mono-hull. For a family group in their 60s, that's the difference between a lovely hour on the water and an hour spent regretting lunch.

Grande Bleu is the only certified catamaran brand in Korea, which matters because certification covers hull safety, crew licensing, and passenger capacity limits. The captain and engineer are both fully licensed — this isn't a small charter run by one person moonlighting.

The vessel holds 91 people across two hulls, but it never feels packed because guests spread between the interior lounge and the outer deck.

What the 60-Minute Route Actually Covers

The Coastal Highlights course sails west from Daepo Port along Seogwipo's volcanic coastline — some of the most dramatic geology in South Korea, part of the UNESCO Global Geopark network.

  • Elephant Rock (Kkokkiribawi) — a cliff naturally shaped like an elephant
  • Jingotnae Sea Canyon — deep vertical cliff walls
  • Wolpyeong Basalt Columns — hexagonal volcanic sea cliffs
  • Wolpyeong Sea Cave — sea cave with crashing surf
  • Pangpaengideok — a wide flat reef platform (photogenic if the light's right)
  • Keunbada Donggul — the largest sea cave on the coast

Seen from land, the Jusangjeolli columns are impressive. Seen from a catamaran at sea level, they're a different beast entirely — you're looking up at 20-metre hexagonal cliffs from the water.

Fishing Onboard (Yes, Really)

Every cruise includes a fishing segment. Rods and bait are provided free, and the crew helps beginners — this is a proper hook-and-line experience, not a demo.

Common catches off the Seogwipo coast:

  • Rockfish (우럭)
  • Scorpionfish (쏨뱅이)
  • Filefish (쥐치)
  • Puffer (복어)

Grandkids tend to love this part. Catch-and-release is standard, and the crew handles anything unfriendly (puffer fish, we're looking at you).

Is It Safe for Kids and Seniors?

Yes — and this is where the catamaran design earns its keep. The twin-hull layout stays flat even in choppier winter swells, and the deck has proper railings throughout. There's a covered interior lounge if the wind picks up, which it can in December and January.

The crew include a licensed engineer, safety briefings happen before every departure, and life jackets are onboard for every guest. Grande Bleu has run 100,000+ guests since 2011 without the reputation issues that plague some cheaper operators.

Boarding is at Daepo Port, which has step-on access — much easier than the wet-boarding you'll find at smaller harbours.

Jeju vs Bali vs the Greek Islands (for a Winter Sailing Trip)

Honest comparison, since you're likely weighing options:

  • Bali: warmer, but December is monsoon season and sailings get cancelled
  • Greek islands: effectively closed November–March
  • Maldives: premium price, long-haul from Hong Kong
  • Jeju in winter: 2.5 hours from HKG, mild temperatures on the south coast, calm winter seas, no cancellations from monsoon

Jeju isn't tropical — bring a light jacket. But for a Jeju winter warm trip that combines sailing, hot springs, and Korean food without a 9-hour flight, it's genuinely underrated.
