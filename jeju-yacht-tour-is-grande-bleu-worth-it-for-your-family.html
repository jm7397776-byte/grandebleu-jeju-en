Uncovering Jeju's Hidden Coastal Gems from the Water

The true magic of a Grande Bleu Jeju yacht tour lies in the unique perspective it offers. From the deck, you'll witness Seogwipo's rugged coastline in a way impossible from land. Our 60-minute route showcases a dramatic blend of natural wonders, offering both breathtaking views and fantastic photo opportunities. This is the slower, more scenic side of K-culture many international travelers miss.

You'll sail past the majestic Wolpyeong Basalt Columns (Wolpyeong Jusangjeolli), a UNESCO-style geological wonder of hexagonal volcanic cliffs rising from the sea. Gaze upon the iconic Elephant Rock (Kkokkiribawi), a natural cliff shaped exactly like its namesake. We also venture near the deep Jingotnae Sea Canyon, the mysterious Wolpyeong Sea Cave, and the broad, photogenic Pangpaengideok reef plateau.

These aren't just pretty sights; they are ancient formations telling the story of Jeju's volcanic past. Seeing them from the ocean truly emphasizes their scale and grandeur. It’s an experience that rivals, yet distinctively differs from, the typical island hopping in Bali or the serene waters of the Maldives or Greek islands, offering a unique Korean charm.

Getting to Grande Bleu Yacht in Seogwipo

The Grande Bleu departs from Daepo Port in Seogwipo, located on the southern coast of Jeju Island. While Jeju's roads can get busy, especially during peak summer months (June, July, August), getting to Daepo Port is relatively straightforward.

We recommend using GPS or a navigation app to guide you directly to "Daepo Port" (대포항). Public parking is available near the port. If you're staying in Seogwipo, the port is usually a short taxi ride away. For those driving from other parts of Jeju Korea, consider taking the coastal routes for scenic views and to potentially avoid some inland traffic.
