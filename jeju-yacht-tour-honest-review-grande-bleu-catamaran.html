Why a Catamaran, Not a Regular Yacht

Here's what nobody tells you about most "yacht tours" in Asia: many are monohulls that pitch and roll enough to ruin the champagne moment. A catamaran sits on two hulls, so the deck stays flat. That matters if you're bringing kids, older parents, or colleagues in business attire who'd rather not stumble around.

Grande Bleu is the only certified catamaran brand in Korea, which is why it's the one 5-star hotels like Shilla and Parnas point their guests to. It's also the only Jeju yacht where regulations allow guests to sail without wearing life jackets throughout — a small detail that changes how your photos look and how relaxed the trip actually feels.

The 60-Minute Coastal Route

Sixty minutes sounds short until you see how much coastline gets covered. The route hugs the Seogwipo shoreline, which is part of a UNESCO-recognised volcanic landscape, and hits several spots you simply can't reach by land.

  • Elephant Rock — a natural cliff that genuinely looks like an elephant drinking from the sea
  • Jingotnae Sea Canyon — sheer vertical walls dropping into the water
  • Wolpyeong Basalt Columns — hexagonal volcanic pillars, same geology as the Giant's Causeway
  • Wolpyeong Sea Cave — waves crashing into a hollowed-out cliff
  • Pangpaengideok — a wide flat reef plateau, oddly photogenic
  • Large Sea Cave (Keunbada Donggul) — the biggest cave on this stretch of coast

In winter (Dec–Feb), the light gets soft earlier in the afternoon, which is genuinely good news for photos. Jeju stays milder than Seoul or most of northern Europe, and the sea rarely gets rough enough to cancel sailings.

Food and Drink: Actually Unlimited

The onboard bar is the part most reviews undersell. Everything below is included in the ticket, no upsell, unlimited refills:

  • Korean draft beer (fresh brew)
  • Red and white wine
  • Fresh-squeezed Jeju tangerine juice
  • Bottled water
  • Jeju local cookies and traditional snacks
  • Korean ramyeon, cooked onboard

If you're travelling as a corporate group, this alone shifts the value equation. A single glass of wine at a Jeju hotel bar runs close to what a discounted child ticket costs here.

Fishing Included (Yes, Really)

Rods and bait are provided free. You'll be targeting Jeju local species — rockfish, scorpionfish, filefish, and puffer. Nobody's guaranteeing you'll catch anything, but the setup is beginner-friendly and kids tend to be riveted. Catch-and-release is available if you'd rather not deal with a fish.

This is the part that makes it a genuine family activity rather than just a scenic ride. Somebody's fishing, somebody's on their second glass of wine, and everyone gets the same sunset.

Best Time to Sail in Winter

Golden hour lands earlier in December and January — often around 16:30–17:00. Book the Sunset Tour, not the earlier Luxury Tour, if photos are the priority. Bring a light jacket even on mild days; the wind off the water adds a chill the forecast won't show.
