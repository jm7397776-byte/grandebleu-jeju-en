Why a Catamaran Beats a Regular Boat

Most Jeju boat tours use single-hull vessels that lean and roll. A catamaran sits on two hulls, so it stays flat even when the swell picks up — a big deal if you're travelling with parents in their 70s or kids prone to motion sickness.

Grande Bleu is the only certified catamaran brand in Korea, which matters for two reasons. First, it's the sole yacht in Jeju rated safe enough to sail without mandatory life vests (they're onboard if you want one). Second, licensed captains and engineers are onboard every sailing — not deckhands with weekend tickets.

Compared to a Bali sunset sail or a Halong Bay cruise, Jeju's appeal is the volcanic coastline. You're not looking at generic tropical water; you're passing UNESCO-grade basalt columns that look sculpted.

The 60-Minute Coastal Route

The route runs west from Daepo Port along Seogwipo's most dramatic stretch of coast. In 60 minutes you cover:

  • Elephant Rock — a cliff shaped, unmistakably, like an elephant drinking
  • Jingotnae Sea Canyon — sheer vertical walls that swallow sound
  • Wolpyeong Basalt Columns — hexagonal volcanic pillars, part of Jeju's UNESCO Global Geopark
  • Wolpyeong Sea Cave and the Large Sea Cave (Keunbada Donggul)
  • Pangpaengideok, a wide flat reef plateau that's oddly photogenic
  • The southern channel where wild dolphins occasionally appear (no guarantees — they're wild)

In October and November, the light angles low across the cliffs by late afternoon. Autumn is genuinely the best window: cooler air, calmer seas than summer's typhoon tail, and Jeju autumn sunset colours that photograph without filters.

  • Korean draft beer (fresh brew)

For a group of colleagues or an extended family, that math works out fast. Two glasses of wine and a beer at any Seogwipo hotel bar already costs more than the difference between a "cheap" boat tour and this one.

Fishing, Included

Rods and bait are provided at no extra cost. Crew set you up regardless of experience — this isn't a serious angling trip, it's a novelty that happens to work. Common catches include rockfish, scorpionfish, filefish and puffer, all local Jeju species. Catch-and-release is the standard, and kids get properly involved rather than watching from the side.
