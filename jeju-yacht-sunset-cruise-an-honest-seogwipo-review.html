Coastal Perspectives: Seeing Wolpyeong Basalt Columns from the Water

The Jusangjeolli cliffs in Seogwipo feature dramatic 20 to 30-meter high hexagonal volcanic rock columns that stretch 2 kilometers along the shore. Viewing this geological formation from the terrestrial wooden viewing decks often comes with tour bus crowds and limited angles.

Sailing out from Daepo Port aboard a Grande Bleu Jeju yacht tour shifts your viewpoint. Looking up at the massive basalt cliffs from sea level reveals structural details impossible to see from land.

During the 60-minute coastal route, the catamaran navigates past several distinct coastal landmarks:

  • Elephant Rock (Kkokkiribawi): A natural sea cliff shaped remarkably like an elephant dipping its trunk into the ocean.
  • Jingotnae Sea Canyon: A narrow coastal inlet framed by sheer vertical rock walls.
  • Wolpyeong Basalt Columns: Hexagonal volcanic sea cliffs formed by cooling lava, recognized under Jeju's UNESCO Global Geopark status.
  • Wolpyeong Sea Cave & Large Sea Cave (Keunbada Donggul): Deep natural sea caverns carved out by centuries of ocean waves.
  • Pangpaengideok: A broad, flat basalt reef plateau sitting right at water level.

During clear autumn afternoons, the golden hour light strikes the dark basalt rocks, creating ideal conditions for photography without needing heavy editing filters.

One common hesitation travelers have about private or small-group cruises is hidden fees. The Grande Bleu experience operates on an all-inclusive refreshment model with unlimited free refills throughout the hour.

  • Snacks: Local Jeju cookies and traditional baked treats.
  • Warm Meal: Korean instant ramyeon cooked fresh on deck—a comforting treat in the crisp autumn ocean breeze.

Midway through the voyage, the captain anchors in a calm bay along the Seogwipo coast for a casual fishing session. Hand-held fishing rods and bait are provided to all guests at no extra charge.

The waters around Daepo Port are rich with local Jeju marine species, including rockfish (우럭), scorpionfish (쏨뱅이), filefish (쥐치), and small pufferfish (복어). Crew members assist beginners and children with baiting hooks and landing fish. Catch-and-release is encouraged to protect the local marine ecosystem.

Practical Details for Your Jeju Friends Trip Yacht Itinerary

For travelers planning a friends trip or multi-generational family holiday in South Korea, vessel stability matters. Small monohull boats tend to roll significantly in sea swells, which can trigger seasickness.

As a twin-hull catamaran, Grande Bleu distributes its 40-ton weight across two parallel hulls. This engineering design dramatically reduces swaying, offering a smooth ride even for passengers who usually get seasick.
