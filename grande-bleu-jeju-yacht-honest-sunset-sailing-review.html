Why a Catamaran, Not Just Any Yacht?

Most "yacht tours" in Asia are single-hull motorboats that rock hard the moment the wind picks up. A catamaran has two hulls, which means it sits flat on the water — much better if you (or your friends) are prone to seasickness.

Grande Bleu is the only certified catamaran brand in Korea, which sounds like marketing talk until you're actually on deck in October chop and realise your wine glass isn't sliding. The 17.1m hull and 40-ton build handle Jeju's autumn swells without drama.

If you're comparing this to a Bali sunset cruise or a Greek islands day-sail, the closest cousin is Santorini — minus the crowds and the €150 price tag.

What You Actually See on the 60-Minute Route

The coastal highlights loop runs west from Daepo Port along Seogwipo's volcanic cliffs. From the water, it's a completely different Jeju than the one you see from Jusangjeolli's tourist boardwalk.

The route passes:

  • Elephant Rock (Kkokkiribawi) — a natural sea stack shaped, unmistakably, like an elephant drinking
  • Jingotnae Sea Canyon — narrow vertical walls carved into the cliffs
  • Wolpyeong Basalt Columns — the hexagonal volcanic sea cliffs (part of Jeju's UNESCO Global Geopark)
  • Wolpyeong Sea Cave — waves crashing straight into the rock
  • Pangpaengideok — a wide, flat reef plateau that photographs beautifully at golden hour
  • Large Sea Cave (Keunbada Donggul) — the biggest cave on this stretch of coast

You've probably seen Jusangjeolli from the clifftop path in Jungmun. From the yacht, you're looking up at those same hexagonal columns from sea level — the scale is genuinely different, and you can't get this angle any other way.

Fishing From the Deck (No Experience Needed)

Every tour includes a fishing experience — rods, tackle, bait, all provided free. The crew sets everything up. If you've never held a rod, they'll walk you through it.

Jeju waters here produce rockfish, scorpionfish, filefish, and puffer. Catch-and-release is standard if you'd rather not deal with the fish. One reviewer on Klook wrote: "I actually caught something — didn't expect that." That reaction is basically the norm, especially with kids.

Speaking of kids: there's no age limit, and under-36-months ride free. Grandparents to toddlers, it works.

Jeju in Autumn: Why October–November Hits Different

Summer Jeju is hot, humid, and crowded. Autumn (September to November) is the quiet season locals prefer — soft light, calmer seas, and pampas grass swaying along the coast.

For the sunset cruise specifically, the Jeju autumn sunset hour drops earlier (around 5:30–6pm in October), so you don't need to stay out late. The light on the basalt cliffs turns gold rather than harsh white, and the crew tells us the water is often glassier this time of year.

For a Jeju friends trip yacht afternoon, autumn is honestly the sweet spot. Fewer tourists, better photos, and you can still sit on deck in a light jacket without freezing.
